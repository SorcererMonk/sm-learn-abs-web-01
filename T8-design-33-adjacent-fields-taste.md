# Adjacent Fields & Taste Development → Training Programme
## T8: Design: 33 · Archive-Building Drills · Cross-Field Studies · Sameness Audits · Product-Decision Reconstructions · Perceived-Quality Trials · Taste Forge

> This is the **training** counterpart to `L8-design-33-adjacent-fields-taste.md`. The L file is the class: what taste is, how it develops, how to build a reference library and vocabulary, twelve adjacent fields to raid, Indian design culture, algorithmic sameness, taste vs trend, taste in product decisions, and the evidence on how users perceive quality. This file is the room afterwards. Nothing here is new material. Everything is repetition, pressure and production, aimed at:
>
> - **Muscle memory:** pulling from an archive instead of a feed by default, articulating a reaction in writing without prompting, and running an options → criteria → choice → consequence structure on a real decision without being told to.
> - **Intuition:** spotting when a "beautiful" choice is trend-following in disguise, feeling the difference between a trained taste judgement and an unexamined preference, and noticing sameness in your own reference pile before someone else points it out.
> - **Taste:** your own cross-field practice — which adjacent fields you actually raid, on what schedule, proven on EcoTrace, Meridian UI or your portfolio and tested with real people, not just admired by you.
>
> Reference: `L#` means that Part of the L file. **Tools:** a notebook or a note-taking app for the articulation log (Part 1); access to at least three archives (Part 2); a phone or camera for analog reference capture; a screen recorder or timer for trials; five willing test participants (classmates, family, TNNLU peers) for the perceived-quality trial (L# 11).

## 1. How to Train

| Shape | Length | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Drill** | 10–20 min | Section 3. Articulation, archive-vs-gallery, and Venn-placement reps. |
| **Exercise** | 45–90 min | Section 4. One graded deliverable (a cross-field study, a decision reconstruction, a trust-signal audit). |
| **Build** | 2–5 h | Section 5. A real reference library, decision log or taste document, moved one milestone. |
| **Trial** | 30–45 min | Section 6. A sameness or trend-following failure, a timer, a diagnosis. |
| **Study** | 30–60 min | Section 7. Deep study of one adjacent-field source, then a written transfer. |
| **Forge** | 30 min, weekly | Section 9. Your own taste statement, updated and tested against this week's work. |

**Rules of the room:**
1. **No new theory during T time.** If a drill needs a concept not in the L file, it's out of scope here.
2. **Every session produces a written artefact:** a log entry, an annotated reference, a decision write-up, logged in `design-training/33-taste/LOG.md`.
3. **Name the source before you use it.** No citing "a moodboard" or "something I saw" — creator, platform, date, or archive and item, every time.
4. **Articulate before you move on.** No reference goes into your library without a one-sentence "why I kept this."
5. **Every decision reconstruction states what was left out**, not just what was chosen (L# 10).
6. **Feed audits are mandatory monthly**, not optional (L# 9).
7. **Log every failure** (Section 12) by mechanism: unexamined trend-following, collecting not absorbing, no named source, aesthetic mistaken for usable, single-field plateau, uncredited cultural borrowing.

**The weekly minimum:** 3 drills · 1 exercise · 1 build session · 1 trial · 1 cross-field study · 1 forge entry · 3 articulation-log entries (any context).

## 4. Exercise Bank

**Level 1 — Seeing and naming taste**
1. **(L# 0)** Write 300 words defining taste vs skill vs opinion, using your own work as the example throughout. *Accept: uses at least one specific, named piece of your own work as evidence, not hypotheticals.*
2. **(L# 0)** Place six pieces (three yours, three admired) on the Art/Craft/Design Venn diagram with one sentence justifying each placement. *Accept: no two justifications are interchangeable — each names something specific to that piece.*

**Level 2 — Building the library**
3. **(L# 1–2)** Start an articulation log; run it daily for one week, minimum one entry a day. *Accept: 7 entries, each a specific "why," none of them a generic aesthetic word ("clean," "nice") used without elaboration.*
4. **(L# 2)** Build a three-archive reference set for a real brief (EcoTrace, Meridian UI or portfolio), each source cited by creator/collection and date, each with a one-line "why kept." *Accept: zero feed sources; all three citable without a screenshot.*

**Level 3 — Cross-field transfer**
5. **(L# 3–4)** Complete the architecture exercise and the film exercise; write 150 words each on what transferred to a real screen in your product. *Accept: names a specific screen and a specific change made because of the exercise.*
6. **(L# 5–6)** Complete the craft exercise (one named, GI-tagged tradition) and the game-design exercise; write the transfer for each. *Accept: craft write-up names the specific tradition, region and constraint set correctly; game write-up names a specific onboarding moment redesigned.*
7. **(L# 7)** Redesign one real or template legal clause as a one-page visual document; test it on someone with no legal training. *Accept: tester can correctly restate the clause's effect after one read; you log exactly where they misread it, if anywhere.*

**Level 4 — Indian context and sameness**
8. **(L# 8)** Audit one real Indian SME or D2C product page for trust signals; classify each as necessary or noise, with justification. *Accept: at least one signal is judged noise and justified — an audit that finds every signal necessary hasn't been critical enough.*
9. **(L# 9)** Audit your last month of reference sources; compute an archive-to-gallery ratio; add one new archive source. *Accept: ratio is stated as a real number, not "mostly," and the new archive source is cited correctly.*

**Level 5 — Decisions and evidence**
10. **(L# 10)** Reconstruct one EcoTrace or Meridian UI decision (real, from your own work) as options → criteria → choice → consequence, stating what was left out. *Accept: at least two options are seriously considered, not a strawman and the real choice; the "left out" is specific, not "other stuff."*
11. **(L# 10)** Apply the taste-vs-trend decidable test to three of your own past choices; for at least one, be honest that it was trend-following. *Accept: at least one of the three is judged trend-following, with the specific trend named.*
12. **(L# 11)** Run the polished-vs-plain perceived-usability trial with five participants; report satisfaction and task-completion separately. *Accept: the two measures are reported as distinct numbers, and any disagreement between them is named explicitly, not smoothed over.*

**Level 6 — Culture, connections and synthesis**
13. **(L# 5, 8)** Trace one GI-tagged craft's constraint set (palette, tools, repeat unit) from a documented source and write the equivalent constraint budget for a component you're designing. *Accept: source is named and documented, not a general "Indian pattern" reference; the constraint budget is a specific, numbered list.*
14. **(L# 12)** Write your own 200-word taste statement: what you consistently choose to leave out, and why, across at least three shipped pieces. *Accept: references specific shipped work, not aspirational preferences.*

## 6. Critique & Failure Drills

**Trials** (30–45 min each, timed, diagnose the mechanism):
- **Trend-check trial.** Given five screens (yours and others'), apply the taste-vs-trend decidable test to each under time pressure. Log how many you initially misjudged and why.
- **Sameness trial.** Given a set of ten reference images with sources hidden, guess which came from an archive and which from a current feed, based on the work alone. Then reveal sources and diagnose what gave each away.
- **Uncredited-borrowing trial.** Given three mockups using cultural or craft motifs, identify which are correctly named/sourced and which would fail the Part 5/8 citation standard. Rewrite the failing ones with correct attribution.
- **Aesthetic-bias trial.** Given a polished mockup and its actual usability-test data (task completion, errors), predict the data from the mockup alone before looking, then compare your prediction to the real numbers and log the gap.

**Live review protocol:** paste a Figma link or upload 2–3 screens plus your written decision rationale to a Claude chat, with this prompt: *"Review this screen's design decisions against a taste standard, not a style standard: (1) what was probably left out and is it justified, (2) is any choice trend-following without evaluation, (3) is any cultural or craft reference used without being named/sourced, (4) where might perceived polish be masking an actual usability problem. Be specific, not general."* Log the response against your own self-assessment; note every place the review caught something you missed.

**Postmortem template** (for any failed trial or exercise):
```
Date:
Artefact:
What I claimed (before checking): 
What was actually true (after checking):
Mechanism of failure (unexamined trend / collecting not absorbing / no named source /
  aesthetic mistaken for usable / single-field plateau / uncredited borrowing / other):
What I'll change next time:
```

## 7. Study & Reproduce

**Weekly cycle:**
1. **Choose one adjacent field** (Parts 3–7) you haven't studied this month — rotate deliberately, don't default to the one you're most comfortable with.
2. **Study one named, credited source** in that field for 30–45 minutes (an archive item, a documented craft tradition, a cited film scene, a real legal clause) — not a feed scroll.
3. **Articulate in writing** what the source is doing and why, before describing what it looks like (L# 5's fine-art discipline, generalised).
4. **Reproduce or transfer.** Either recreate a constrained version of the technique, or apply the specific principle to a real screen in EcoTrace or Meridian UI.
5. **Log the transfer** in your study log with the source cited correctly.

**Target list (rotate through across a quarter, minimum one per adjacent field):**
- Architecture: one building's procession, diagrammed.
- Fashion/textiles: one collection or one Indian textile tradition, constraint-mapped.
- Film: one scene, six-frame storyboarded, muted.
- Photography: ten light-only variations of one object.
- Music: one piece, silence-mapped against a screen.
- Fine art: one archive artwork, question-before-appearance write-up.
- Craft: one GI-tagged Indian tradition, constraint-traced from a documented source.
- Literature/poetry: one piece of your product's copy, compressed to five lines and back.
- Game design: one game's first five minutes, mechanic-taught-without-instruction, mapped.
- Product/industrial design: one physical object, Rams-checklist audit.
- Science/engineering: one feature, hypothesis stated before design.
- Law: one real or template clause, visually redesigned and comprehension-tested.

## 8. Intuition Probes

- Shown a screen with no context, guess in 10 seconds whether its dominant visual choice (layering, a colour, a motion style) was taste-driven or trend-following — then check against any rationale available. How often are you right?
- Shown two versions of the same task flow, one visibly more polished, predict which will score higher on task completion before any data exists. Track your prediction accuracy over ten trials — if you're consistently wrong, the aesthetic–usability effect is biasing your own judgement, not just users' (L# 11).
- Given a craft motif in a mockup with the source deliberately hidden, guess whether it's correctly named/sourced or generic. What gave it away, if anything?
- Given a paragraph of legal or policy text, guess in 15 seconds whether it would pass a plain-language readability check, then verify.
- After a month of archive-only referencing, notice whether your own default "what does good look like" has shifted — write down, unprompted, what's different.

## 9. Taste Forge

Weekly, 30 minutes, no exceptions:
1. **Update your taste statement** (Exercise 14) with anything from this week's work that confirms, complicates or contradicts it.
2. **Name one thing you left out this week** and why — from a real screen, not a hypothetical.
3. **Name one adjacent-field source you consumed this week that wasn't design** — if you can't name one, that's the finding.
4. **Revisit one piece of your own work from three months ago.** Note plainly whether it still holds up, and why or why not.
5. **Log one place your own taste was wrong this week** — a choice you'd now make differently, and what changed your mind (new evidence, new source, a user's reaction).

The forge is not a mood log. Every entry must name a specific artefact, source or decision — "I feel like my taste is improving" is not an entry.

## 10. Assessment & Graduation

Graduate this module when you can produce, on demand:
1. **A maintained reference library** of at least six archive sources across at least four adjacent fields, each with a correct citation and a written "why kept" (L# 2).
2. **A cross-field study log** covering all twelve adjacent fields from Parts 3–7, each with a documented transfer to real work.
3. **Three fully reconstructed product decisions** (EcoTrace, Meridian UI or portfolio) in options → criteria → choice → consequence form, each explicitly stating what was left out (L# 10).
4. **One completed perceived-vs-actual usability trial** with real participant data, satisfaction and task-completion reported separately, and any disagreement between them named (L# 11).
5. **One visual, plain-language legal-clause redesign**, comprehension-tested on a non-lawyer reader (L# 7).
6. **One trust-signal audit** of a real Indian product page, with at least one signal judged necessary and one judged noise, justified (L# 8).
7. **A written personal taste statement**, revised at least once from its first draft, grounded in named shipped work (L# 12).
8. **A clean Black-belt gate** (Section 2), including the six-adjacent-field minimum and the documented perceived-quality trial.
