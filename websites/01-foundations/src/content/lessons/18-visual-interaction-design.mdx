## The role: the design engineer

A designer-developer (design engineer) closes the gap between intent and implementation: making design decisions in code, knowing when a design won't survive real content, and being fluent in both Figma and CSS. The superpower is **iteration speed without a handoff**; the obligation is **design judgement**, not just implementation fidelity.

<Try task="Take a design you've been handed (or any Figma community file). List five places where it won't survive real content or real devices, and propose a design decision for each, not just an implementation.">
  Common finds: a card title that wraps to three lines in German; a price that doesn't fit when it has seven digits; an avatar row that breaks with 50 members; a hero image cropped badly on a phone; a hover-only menu with no touch equivalent. For each, propose the design answer (clamp titles to two lines with a tooltip, tabular numbers and a smaller currency, "+47 more", art direction, a tap target), then build it.
</Try>

## Perception and Gestalt

**Proximity** (the most powerful grouping tool, so spacing *is* structure), **similarity**, **common region** (cards and backgrounds), **continuity**, **closure**, **figure–ground**, **common fate** (things that move together belong together), and **focal point**.

## Visual hierarchy

Levers: size, weight, colour and value contrast, position, space and depth. **De-emphasise secondary content instead of shouting the primary** (the *Refactoring UI* lesson). Test with the **squint test** and the **greyscale test**. One primary action per view.

## Space and layout

A **spacing scale** (a 4/8-point base or a geometric scale) used exclusively; more whitespace than feels comfortable at first; **internal ≤ external** (space inside a group is smaller than space between groups); alignment to few edges; grids (columns, gutters, margins; 12 columns as a tool, not a religion); **optical vs mathematical alignment** (icons and round shapes need nudging); and **composition**: balance, asymmetry, tension, rhythm, the rule of thirds, and reading flow (F- and Z-patterns).

## Interaction design and component states

**Affordances and signifiers** (Norman): what looks clickable must be clickable, and vice versa. Feedback for every action, natural mapping, constraints. And design **every state** of every component: default, hover, focus, active/pressed, selected, disabled, loading, error, success, empty, skeleton, read-only, dragged, and "long content".

## UX laws and heuristics

Use them as lenses, not rules. **Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics**; **Fitts's law** (big, close targets for frequent actions; screen edges are infinitely deep); **Hick's law** (fewer choices, progressive disclosure); **Jakob's law** (users spend most of their time on *other* sites, so follow conventions); the aesthetic-usability effect; **Tesler's law** (complexity is conserved: someone has to deal with it); the **Doherty threshold** (feedback under 400 ms); the peak-end rule; the serial-position effect; and cognitive load.

<Try task="Pick one app you use daily. Find one violation each of Fitts's, Hick's and Jakob's laws, and one Nielsen heuristic, with a proposed fix for each.">
  Examples: Fitts: a tiny "×" close button in a corner far from the thumb (make it 44px or add swipe-to-dismiss). Hick: a settings page with 40 toggles on one screen (group and progressively disclose). Jakob: a logo that doesn't link home, or a cart icon on the left (follow the convention). Nielsen #1, visibility of system status: an upload with no progress indicator (add a determinate progress bar and an `aria-live` message).
</Try>

## Information architecture and navigation

Content inventories, **card sorting**, hierarchies and labels, navigation patterns (top bar, sidebar, tabs, a bottom tab bar on mobile, breadcrumbs, mega menus, **command palettes**), wayfinding (you-are-here signals with `aria-current`), and search vs browse.

<Try task="Run a five-minute open card sort on paper: write 15 features of a product on cards, group them without labels, then name each group. Compare your groups with the product's actual navigation.">
  You'll usually find the product's navigation mirrors its org chart ("Admin", "Workspace settings", "Account") while your groups follow tasks ("Invite people", "Pay", "Get help"). Task-based labels and fewer top-level items (5–7) are the typical fix; put rarely used items in a command palette and search.
</Try>

## UX writing and microcopy

Buttons say what they do ("Save changes", not "OK"); **error messages say what happened and how to fix it**; empty states teach the next step; onboarding copy is short; tone of voice is consistent. And **real content in designs**: lorem ipsum hides every layout problem.

<Try task="Rewrite these: a button 'Submit', an error 'Invalid input', an empty state 'No data', and a confirmation dialog 'Are you sure?' with buttons 'Yes' / 'No'.">
  "Create account"; "Enter a date in the future, like 12/10/2026"; "No invoices yet. When a customer pays, their invoice appears here. [Create an invoice]"; "Delete 'Atlas'? This removes 42 files for everyone and can't be undone." with buttons "Delete project" / "Keep project". Verbs name the outcome, errors tell people how to recover, and buttons still make sense when read alone.
</Try>

## Forms and data display as design problems

Single-column forms; label placement above fields; **input widths that hint expected length** (a postcode field is short); sensible defaults. Tables designed for **scanning**: text left, **numbers right with tabular figures**, zebra stripes vs rules, density modes. Charts with honest scales and **direct labels** instead of legends. Dashboards with a hierarchy.

## Responsive and adaptive design thinking

Content priority per context; touch vs pointer; **one-handed mobile use** (thumb zones: primary actions within reach at the bottom); and designing the **smallest and largest layouts first**, then filling in between.

<Try task="Sketch the smallest (320px) and largest (1920px) versions of one screen before any middle size. Where does the primary action live on each, and can a thumb reach it on the phone?">
  On the phone, move the primary action to a bottom bar or a sticky footer button within the thumb zone, collapse secondary navigation into a menu or tab bar, and stack content by priority. On the large screen, use the width for a second column (context, preview, filters), not wider paragraphs; keep the measure readable and the primary action near the content it acts on.
</Try>

## Design process for one person

Brief → research (collect 20 references in a **swipe file**) → sketches (paper is fastest) → low-fidelity wireframes → a visual direction on one key screen (a **style tile** or element collage) → build in the browser → critique → iterate. Evidence: **usability testing with five users** (Nielsen), hallway testing, and A/B tests.

<Try task="Make a style tile for a fictional brand in 30 minutes: one headline + body type pairing, a 5-colour palette, one button, one card, a texture or image treatment, and three adjectives for the voice.">
  A good tile answers "what does this brand feel like?" without designing a page: e.g. "calm, precise, warm" → Fraunces headlines with Instrument Sans body, stone neutrals with one terracotta accent, a softly rounded button with an inner highlight, a card with layered shadows, and subtle grain. Validate it by showing two tiles to someone and asking which fits the adjectives.
</Try>

## Figma fluency for developers

| Figma | CSS / code |
| --- | --- |
| Frame + constraints | A positioned container; constraints ≈ `inset` values |
| **Auto layout** | Flexbox (hug = `fit-content`, fill = `flex: 1`) |
| Layout grids, guides | CSS grid with named lines |
| Components, variants, properties | A component API (props, `data-variant`) |
| **Variables and modes** | Design tokens and themes (custom properties, `[data-theme]`) |
| Styles | Shared text/effect tokens |
| Dev Mode, inspect | Specs; verify against tokens, never copy raw values |
| Plugins | Contrast checkers, token exporters |

<Try task="Open any Figma community UI kit. Find one component and write down: its variants and properties (the API), which auto-layout settings map to which flex properties, and which values should be tokens but aren't.">
  E.g. a Button with variants Type (primary/secondary/ghost), Size (sm/md/lg), State (default/hover/disabled) and a boolean Icon property → `data-variant`, `data-size`, CSS states from pseudo-classes, and a slot. Auto layout horizontal, gap 8, padding 12/16, hug → `display: inline-flex; gap: .5rem; padding: .75rem 1rem`. Hard-coded hex fills and 13px text that should be tokens get flagged back to design.
</Try>

## Design critique

Give and receive critique **objective-led**: "does this achieve the goal?", not "I like it". Use the vocabulary: hierarchy, rhythm, contrast, balance, alignment, density, consistency. Critique your own work after a break, and defend decisions with **reasons**, not taste claims.

<Try task="Critique one screen you built last month using this format: the goal, what works (with the principle), what doesn't (with the principle), one change you'd make first and why.">
  "Goal: get a new user to create their first project. Works: the empty state has one clear action (Hick's law) and friendly copy. Doesn't: the heading, the helper text and the button have similar weight (weak hierarchy), and the illustration pulls the eye first (focal point on the wrong thing). First change: de-emphasise the illustration and helper text so the button is the only high-contrast element."
</Try>

## Brand and identity basics

Logo usage (clear space, minimum size, allowed colourways), a brand's **type and colour system**, **voice**, and turning a brand guide into **tokens and components**, so the brand is enforced by code rather than by memory.

<Try task="Take any public brand guide (many are online as PDFs). Convert its colours, type and spacing rules into a small DTCG token file with primitive and semantic tiers.">
  Primitives come straight from the guide (`color.brand.red.500: #e0301e`, `font.family.display: "Neue Haas"`), semantic tokens give them jobs (`color.action.primary → {color.brand.red.500}`, `color.text.default`), and rules that can't be tokens (logo clear space, "never place the logo on red") become component constraints and docs.
