## Colour science for CSS

The eye perceives **lightness**, **chroma** (colourfulness) and **hue**. A **colour space** is a coordinate system; a **gamut** is the range a device can show (sRGB < Display-P3 < Rec. 2020). **HSL is not perceptually uniform**: a yellow and a blue at the same HSL lightness look nothing alike. **OKLab/OKLCH** fix that: equal steps in L look equal, chroma is predictable, and hue doesn't drift as you lighten or darken. That's why they're the modern authoring spaces.

## Wide gamut

Many screens show **Display-P3**, about 25% more colours than sRGB, mostly vivid greens, oranges and reds. Author P3 colours with `oklch()` (high chroma) and serve them inside `@media (color-gamut: p3)`. Out-of-gamut colours are **gamut-mapped** by the browser (reducing chroma until they fit), which is why vivid brand colours look different on different screens.

## Colour functions: mix, derive, switch

**`color-mix(in oklch, var(--a) 30%, var(--b))`** mixes in a chosen space (the space matters: sRGB mixes go muddy). **Relative colour syntax** derives variants from one token: `oklch(from var(--brand) calc(l + 0.1) c h)`. **`light-dark(light, dark)`** with `color-scheme` themes in one declaration. **`contrast-color()`** picks accessible text for any background automatically. System colours (`Canvas`, `CanvasText`…) follow the user's platform.

## Colour theory for designers

Hue, saturation, **value**: value contrast carries hierarchy (the squint test). Harmonies (monochromatic, analogous, complementary, split-complementary, triadic) are starting points, not rules. **60-30-10**: a dominant neutral, a secondary, a small accent. Colour psychology and cultural meaning, held with humility. The **brand colour is rarely right** for large surfaces or body text. And restraint: **one accent**.

## Contrast and accessibility

**WCAG 2** contrast ratio: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text (24px, or 18.66px bold), UI component boundaries and focus indicators (1.4.11). Its known flaws: it overrates dark-on-dark and underrates some light-on-dark pairs (white on orange). **APCA**, proposed for WCAG 3, models perception better: use it as a design aid, and meet WCAG 2 for compliance. Never use colour alone to convey meaning (1.4.1).

## Dark mode done properly

`color-scheme` for native controls and scrollbars; `prefers-color-scheme` plus a **user toggle** that persists and doesn't flash (a blocking inline script or a server-side cookie: this site uses the script). A **separate dark palette**, not inversion: lower saturation, **avoid pure black** for large surfaces, **raise elevated surfaces with lighter colours** (shadows barely show on dark), re-check every contrast pair. Dim photos slightly, swap illustrations with `<picture>` and `prefers-color-scheme`, and keep it in one place with `light-dark()`.

## Forced colours and high contrast

In Windows' contrast themes, **`forced-colors: active`** replaces your colours with the user's system colours, removes background images and box shadows, and makes borders reappear. Use **system colour keywords** (`Canvas`, `CanvasText`, `LinkText`, `ButtonFace`, `ButtonText`, `Highlight`, `HighlightText`, `GrayText`, `AccentColor`), give custom controls **transparent borders** (they become visible), use `currentColor` in SVG icons, and reserve `forced-color-adjust: none` for rare cases like colour swatches.

## Gradients

`linear-gradient`, `radial-gradient` (shape, size keywords like `closest-side`, position), **`conic-gradient`** (pie charts, colour wheels, loaders) and the `repeating-` variants for stripes. Colour stops and **hints** shape transitions. **Interpolate in OKLCH** (`linear-gradient(in oklch, …)`) to avoid the grey dead zone of sRGB; `longer hue` for rainbows. **Easing gradients** (scrims) make text legible over images without a visible edge. Fight banding with a little noise, and fake **mesh gradients** with stacked radials.

## Shadows and depth

`box-shadow` (offset, blur, spread, inset, several at once). **Layered shadows** look real: several shadows with increasing blur and decreasing opacity, **tinted with the background hue**, never pure black. Keep one **light source** and a consistent **elevation scale**. `text-shadow` helps legibility on images; **`filter: drop-shadow()`** follows alpha shapes (cut-out images, SVG).
