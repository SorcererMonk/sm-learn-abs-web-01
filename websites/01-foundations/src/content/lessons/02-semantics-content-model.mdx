## Content categories and content models

Every element belongs to **content categories** (metadata, flow, sectioning, heading, phrasing, embedded, interactive, palpable) and has a **content model**: the categories its children may belong to. `<p>` accepts phrasing content only; `<ul>` accepts only `<li>` (and script-supporting elements). Some elements are **transparent** (`<a>`, `<ins>`, `<del>`, `<map>`): their model is their parent's, which is why `<a>` can wrap a whole card in a flow context. And **interactive content cannot nest**: no `<button>` in an `<a>`, no `<a>` in an `<a>`.

## Document structure and landmarks

`<header>`, `<nav>`, `<main>` (one visible per page), `<aside>`, `<footer>`, `<section>` (needs a heading, and becomes a *region* landmark only when named), `<article>` (self-contained, syndicatable), `<search>` and `<address>` (contact information, not postal addresses in general). Each maps to an ARIA landmark role, and screen-reader users jump between landmarks like chapters.

## Headings are the real outline

`<h1>`–`<h6>` levels **are** the document's structure. The "document outline algorithm" that would have derived levels from nested `<section>`s was never implemented and has been removed from the spec. So: one `<h1>` per page is a sound convention, don't skip levels, use `<hgroup>` to group a heading with its subtitle, and **never choose a level for its size**: style is CSS's job.

## Text-level semantics

Each element has a real meaning: `<em>` (stress) vs `<i>` (alternate voice), `<strong>` (importance) vs `<b>` (attention), `<small>` (side comments, fine print), `<s>` (no longer accurate) vs `<del>`/`<ins>` (edits, with `datetime`), `<u>` (rarely right), `<mark>` (relevance highlight), `<q>` and `<blockquote cite>` with `<cite>` (the *title of a work*), `<abbr title>`, `<dfn>`, `<code>`, `<kbd>`, `<samp>`, `<var>`, `<pre>`, **`<time datetime>`**, `<data value>`, `<sub>`/`<sup>`, `<br>` (line breaks that belong to the content: poems, addresses), `<wbr>`, `<bdi>`/`<bdo>`, and `<ruby>`/`<rt>`/`<rp>`.

## Grouping content

`<ul>`, `<ol>` (with `start`, `reversed`, `type`), `<li value>`, `<dl>`/`<dt>`/`<dd>` for key–value groups (you may wrap each group in a `<div>`), `<figure>`/`<figcaption>` (any self-contained content with a caption, not only images), `<hr>` (a *thematic* break, not a line), `<div>` (the element of last resort, fine when nothing else applies) and `<menu>` (a list of commands, semantically a `<ul>`).

## Links

`href` can be absolute, relative, root-relative, `mailto:`, `tel:`, `sms:`, a fragment `#id` or a **text fragment** `#:~:text=…`. `target="_blank"` now implies `noopener`. `rel` values carry meaning: `noopener`, `noreferrer`, `nofollow`, `ugc`, `sponsored`, `me`, `external`, `author`, `license`, `prev`/`next`. Also `download`, `hreflang`, `type`, `ping`, `referrerpolicy`. And the rule that matters most: **a link goes somewhere and changes the URL; a button does something.** Link text must make sense out of context: "Read the pricing guide", never "click here".

```html
<a href="/guides/pricing">Read the pricing guide</a>
<a href="tel:+919876543210">Call +91 98765 43210</a>
<a href="https://example.com/article#:~:text=cycle%20lanes">Jump to the cycle lanes quote</a>
<a href="/report.pdf" download>Download the report (PDF, 2 MB)</a>
<a href="https://sponsor.example" rel="sponsored noopener" target="_blank">Our sponsor (opens in a new tab)</a>
```

## Tables for tabular data

Use `<caption>`, `<thead>`/`<tbody>`/`<tfoot>`, `<th scope="col|row|colgroup|rowgroup">`, `headers`/`id` for complex tables, `<colgroup>`/`<col>` for column styling, `abbr` on headers, and `aria-sort` on sortable headers. On small screens: horizontal scroll inside a labelled, focusable region; reflow to cards; or prioritised columns. Never tables for layout (except in email, Part 23).

## Sectioning in practice

A **page shell** (header, nav, main, footer). **Article cards** with the heading inside and *one* primary link, extended to cover the card with the "block link" pattern (a pseudo-element on the link, not a link wrapping everything, so text stays selectable and the accessible name stays short). Repeated components named with `aria-labelledby`. **Breadcrumbs** as `<nav aria-label="Breadcrumb">` with an `<ol>` and `aria-current="page"` on the last item.

## Microformats and machine-readable semantics

Machines read meaning two ways: **microformats** (`h-card`, `h-entry` classes, used across the IndieWeb) and **JSON-LD** (Part 6). Microformats reuse your visible markup; JSON-LD is a separate data island that must match the visible content.

```html
<article class="h-entry">
  <h1 class="p-name">Cycle lanes approved</h1>
  <p>By <a class="p-author h-card" href="/staff/asha">Asha Rao</a>,
     <time class="dt-published" datetime="2026-09-26">26 Sep 2026</time></p>
  <div class="e-content"><p>The council voted…</p></div>
</article>
```
