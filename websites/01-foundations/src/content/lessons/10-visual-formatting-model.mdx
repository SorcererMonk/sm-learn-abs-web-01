## The box model

Every box has **content, padding, border and margin**. With the default **`box-sizing: content-box`**, `width` sets only the content, so padding and border are added on top; **`border-box`** makes `width` include them (the sane choice, set in every reset). `outline` draws outside the border with no layout effect (`outline-offset` moves it). And `width: auto` means different things: blocks **fill** the containing block, while floats, absolutely positioned elements and inline-blocks **shrink-wrap** their content.

## `display` has two values

`display` sets an **outer** type (how the box participates in its parent: `block`, `inline`, `run-in`) and an **inner** type (how it lays out its children: `flow`, `flow-root`, `flex`, `grid`, `table`, `ruby`). `display: flex` is shorthand for `block flex`; `inline-flex` is `inline flex`. Also `list-item` (adds a marker), **`contents`** (the box disappears; its children are promoted), and `none`.

## Normal flow: block and inline formatting contexts

A **block formatting context** (BFC) is a region where blocks stack vertically. New BFCs come from `display: flow-root`, `overflow` other than `visible`/`clip`, floats, absolute positioning, flex and grid items, and `contain: layout`. A BFC **contains its floats**, **stops margin collapsing through it**, and **doesn't overlap floats**. Inside blocks, **inline formatting contexts** lay text out in line boxes aligned on the **baseline**. Images are inline by default and sit on the baseline, leaving space for descenders below them: the "mysterious gap under images". The **strut** (an invisible zero-width character with the parent's font and line-height) is why an empty line still has height.

## Margin collapsing

Vertical margins **collapse** (the larger wins, not the sum) between adjacent siblings, between a parent and its first/last child when nothing (border, padding, inline content, a BFC) separates them, and through empty blocks. They **never collapse horizontally, never in flex or grid containers, and never through a BFC**. That explains "why is there space above my header": the child's margin escaped through the parent. Modern layout avoids relying on it with `gap`, the owl selector and stack layouts.

## Sizing and intrinsic keywords

`width`/`height`, and `min-*`/`max-*`, which **beat** `width`/`height`. **Logical** `inline-size`/`block-size` follow the writing mode. **Intrinsic keywords**: `min-content` (the narrowest without overflow: the longest word), `max-content` (no wrapping at all), `fit-content` (max-content clamped to the available space), `fit-content(20rem)`, and `stretch` **[Frontier]**. **`aspect-ratio`** gives boxes a shape. And percentage heights need a **definite** parent height, which is the whole "`height: 100%` doesn't work" family of bugs.

## Overflow and scroll containers

`visible`, `hidden`, **`clip`** (clips without creating a scroll container, so it doesn't break `position: sticky`), `scroll` and `auto`, set per axis (a `visible` paired with anything else is coerced to `auto`). `overflow-clip-margin` extends the clip. Scroll containers become containing blocks for sticky and anchors. **`scrollbar-gutter: stable`** reserves space so content doesn't shift when a scrollbar appears; `scrollbar-width`/`scrollbar-color` style it; `text-overflow: ellipsis` needs `overflow` and `white-space: nowrap`; **`overscroll-behavior: contain`** stops scroll chaining from a modal or drawer to the page behind.

## The containing block

The concept most developers never learn. For static and relative elements, the containing block is the **nearest block container's content box**. For **`absolute`**, it's the nearest **positioned** ancestor's padding box. For **`fixed`**, it's the viewport, **unless an ancestor has `transform`, `filter`, `perspective`, `contain: paint`/`layout`, `backdrop-filter` or `will-change` of those**. That's why a fixed element suddenly scrolls with its parent.

## Positioning and the five ways sticky fails

`static` (normal flow), `relative` (offset without affecting flow; establishes a containing block for absolute children), `absolute` (out of flow), `fixed`, and **`sticky`** (relative until a scroll threshold, then fixed within its container). Inset properties: `inset`, `inset-inline`, `inset-block`. Centre anything with `position: absolute; inset: 0; margin: auto` plus a size.

**Sticky fails when**: (1) no inset (`top`) is set; (2) an ancestor has `overflow` other than `visible`/`clip`; (3) the sticky element is as tall as its container; (4) a flex/grid parent stretches it (`align-items: stretch`); (5) table-part quirks.

## Anchor positioning **[Frontier → Baseline-bound]**

Tether one element to another in pure CSS: **`anchor-name: --x`** on the anchor, **`position-anchor: --x`** on the positioned element, then either the **`anchor()`** function in inset properties (`top: anchor(bottom)`) and `anchor-size()`, or the **`position-area`** shorthand (a 3×3 grid around the anchor: `top`, `bottom span-right`, …). **`position-try-fallbacks: flip-block, flip-inline`** (or named `@position-try` rules) handle collisions; `position-visibility` hides it when the anchor scrolls away. Popovers and `interestfor` create implicit anchors. A polyfill covers older engines. This replaces Floating UI/Popper for tooltips, menus and callouts.

## Stacking contexts and `z-index`

`z-index` only applies to positioned elements (and flex/grid items), and only **within the nearest stacking context**. Stacking contexts are created by the root, `position` + `z-index`, `opacity < 1`, `transform`, `filter`, `mix-blend-mode`, **`isolation: isolate`**, `will-change`, `contain: paint`/`layout`, `container-type: size`, `backdrop-filter`, `mask`, `clip-path`, and fixed/sticky positioning. Inside a context, painting goes: background → negative z → in-flow blocks → floats → inline content → z-index auto/0 positioned → positive z. `z-index: 9999` "doesn't work" because it's trapped in a parent's context. `isolation: isolate` creates a context deliberately. The **top layer** (dialogs, popovers, fullscreen) sits above all of them.

## Floats today: wrapping and shapes

Floats are no longer a layout system. They're for **wrapping text around things**: images, pull quotes, drop caps. **`shape-outside`** (with `shape-margin`) wraps text around a circle, polygon or an image's alpha channel for editorial layouts. `clear` pushes content below floats.

## Containment and `content-visibility`

`contain` is a promise that lets the browser skip work: **`layout`** (nothing inside affects layout outside), **`paint`** (nothing paints outside the box), **`size`** (the size doesn't depend on children), `inline-size`, `style` (counters and quotes stay inside), and the shorthands `content` (layout + paint + style) and `strict` (+ size). **`content-visibility: auto`** skips rendering off-screen sections entirely until they approach the viewport; pair it with **`contain-intrinsic-size: auto 500px`** so the scrollbar doesn't jump (Part 21).

## Tables as a layout algorithm

The table algorithm is its own formatting model. **`table-layout: fixed`** sizes columns from the first row (fast, predictable, great for big data tables); `auto` looks at every cell's content. `border-collapse: collapse` merges borders; `border-spacing` separates them. **Sticky headers**: make each `<th>` sticky (not the `<thead>`), inside a scroll container. `display: table` for non-table layout is a legacy technique.

## Writing modes and logical properties

`writing-mode` (`horizontal-tb`, `vertical-rl`, `vertical-lr`) and `direction` change which way the **inline** and **block** axes run. **Logical properties** name sides by flow, not by the screen: `margin-inline-start`, `padding-block`, `inset-inline-end`, `border-start-start-radius`, `inline-size`. Write them by default and layouts work in RTL and vertical scripts automatically.
