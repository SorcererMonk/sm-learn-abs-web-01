## Separate the timescales

HTML semantics and the cascade **compound over decades**; layout algorithms and design fundamentals over a **career**; CSS features arrive **yearly** (and become permanent once shipped); frameworks and methodologies rotate every **few years**; aesthetic trends every **season**. Invest in that proportion.

## Participation

**File browser bugs** with reduced test cases (engines fix well-reported bugs), **comment on CSSWG issues** (the working group reads them), **contribute to MDN**, write up techniques with demos, and give talks about something you built.

## Portfolio evidence that carries weight

A **personal site that is itself the proof** (fast, accessible, beautiful, original); **case studies showing process** (problem → constraints → explorations → decision → result, with numbers); a **design system or component library with docs**; recreations and experiments (a CodePen collection with craft); an **accessibility audit and fix**; and **open-source contributions**.

<Try task="Draft the outline of one case study from your own work in the problem → constraints → explorations → decision → result format, with at least two numbers.">
  "Problem: 38% of mobile users abandoned checkout at the address step. Constraints: two weeks, existing React app, WCAG 2.2 AA, no backend changes. Explorations: (a) a multi-step wizard, (b) a single page with autofill, (c) address lookup; (c) rejected because of API cost. Decision: single page with correct autocomplete tokens, inline validation with :user-invalid, and an error summary. Result: abandonment fell to 24%, completion time from 94 s to 51 s, zero axe violations." Add one screenshot of the before and after, and one detail of craft.
</Try>

## Interviews

Build a component live (a **modal, tabs, a responsive card grid**) with accessibility and edge cases; explain **the cascade, specificity, stacking contexts, and the flex and grid algorithms**; **debug a broken layout**; critique a design; talk through a portfolio piece's decisions; and hold design-system and architecture discussions.

## Collaboration

With **designers**: speak Figma, push back with prototypes, not opinions. With **backend engineers**: HTML contracts, forms, APIs. With **content writers**: real content early. With **accessibility and legal teams**: involve them before launch, not after.

<Try task="A designer hands you a card with a fixed 280px height and three lines of title. Write the message you'd send, including a prototype link, that pushes back without an opinion war.">
  "I built the card with real titles from our CMS (link). About 15% of titles wrap to four lines in German and get cut off at 280px. Two options in the prototype: (a) min-height 280px so cards grow, with subgrid keeping rows aligned; (b) clamp titles to three lines with the full title in the link's accessible name. (a) keeps all content visible; (b) keeps the grid rigid. Which matters more for this page?" A prototype plus a question moves the decision to the right person.
</Try>

## Staying current without churn

Follow the platform (release notes, **Baseline**, **Interop**), try new features **in demos before production**, adopt them **with fallbacks** when they reach "newly available", and keep one **"platform-only" project** where you test everything without a framework. (This site is one.)

<Try task="Pick one feature from this course marked [Frontier]. Build a 30-line demo, write its Baseline status and fallback, and decide whether you'd ship it today.">
  E.g. customisable `<select>`: the demo uses `appearance: base-select` inside `@supports`, and the fallback is the native select, fully functional. Baseline: not yet in all engines at the time of writing (check the current status). Ship it today? Yes, because the fallback is the normal select: progressive enhancement means frontier CSS can ship early when failure is harmless.
</Try>
