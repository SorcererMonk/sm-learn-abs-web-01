# Mindset & Philosophy → Training Programme
## T8: Design: 01 · Framing Drills · Critique Trials · Ideation Sprints · Rationale Builds · Taste Forge

> This is the **training** counterpart to `L8-design-01-mindset-philosophy.md`. The L file is the class: it explains what design is, how designers reason and where taste comes from. This file is the room afterwards. Nothing here is new material. Everything is repetition, pressure and production, aimed at three outcomes:
>
> - **Muscle memory:** writing a sharp problem statement, a success criterion and three different directions *before touching a canvas*, fast enough that it stops feeling like homework.
> - **Intuition:** seeing within seconds whether a brief is mis-framed, whether a critique is a reaction or a diagnosis, and whether an idea is your first reflex or an actual choice.
> - **Taste:** your own design philosophy, written down, defended with reasons you discovered and revised only against evidence.
>
> Reference: `L#` below means that Part of the L file. **Tool:** exercises run in **FigJam** (framing, ideation, critique boards) and **Figma** (any visual output). Where an exercise needs a Figma skill, the required belt from `T8-design-34-figma.md` is named, e.g. *[Figma: White]*.

---

## 1. How to Train

**Session shapes** (pick by the time you have, not by mood):

| Shape | Length | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Drill** | 15–30 min | Section 3. Timed, repeated, from memory: framing, critique conversion, ideation bursts. |
| **Exercise** | 30–60 min | Section 4. One graded exercise with a written acceptance check. |
| **Build** | 1–4 h | Section 5. Move one project forward by one milestone, rationale included. |
| **Trial** | 30–45 min | Section 6. A flawed brief or design, a timer, a written critique or postmortem. |
| **Rebuild** | 45–90 min | Section 7. Study a masterwork's reasoning, reconstruct its brief and decisions, diff against the real case study. |
| **Forge** | 30–45 min, weekly | Section 9. Write, revise and defend your own design philosophy. |

**Rules of the room:**
1. **No new theory during T time.** If you need to be taught, that's an L session. Note it and move on.
2. **Every session ends with a committed artefact:** a FigJam board, a Figma frame link, or a Markdown file, named `YYYY-MM-DD-shape-topic`.
3. **Problem statement first, always.** No session that produces a visual may start without a written one-line problem and a success criterion at the top of the board. A visual with no problem statement above it doesn't count, however good it looks. (This is the mindset equivalent of "sanitizers always on".)
4. **Predict before you check.** Before a test, a critique or a reveal, write what you expect. Track your hit rate (Section 8).
5. **Log every failure** (Section 12): mis-frames, ideas you defended and then dropped, critiques you misread.
6. **Timed means timed.** Set the timer, stop when it rings, record the result even if incomplete.
7. **One folder, forever.** `design-training/01-mindset/` with `drills/`, `exercises/`, `builds/`, `trials/`, `rebuilds/`, `forge/`, plus one FigJam file `01-Mindset-Training` with one page per section, and one `LOG.md` shared across all Phase 1 modules.

**The weekly minimum** (scale the hours, keep the ratio): 3 drills · 2 exercises · 1 build session · 1 trial · 1 rebuild · 1 forge entry, plus the **daily 5-minute frustration log** (L# 3).

## 2. The Belt Ladder

Each belt has an **entry gate**: a cold, timed task with no references. Pass it twice on separate days before claiming the belt. If you can't, that belt's drills are your programme.

| Belt | Gate (cold, timed) | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **White** | In 15 min: given a solution-shaped brief ("we need an app for X"), write a problem statement, three stakeholders with their criteria, and one measurable success criterion. | L# 0–1 |
| **Yellow** | In 20 min: produce three *frames* for one brief that lead to three different products, each with a HMW question and a JTBD statement. | L# 1 |
| **Orange** | In 30 min: convert ten reactions/directions into proper critique (objective · element · effect · why), then critique a given screen in writing against its stated goal. | L# 5 |
| **Green** | In 45 min: run a full diverge→converge cycle on a new brief. At least 16 thumbnails (two Crazy 8s rounds), 3 selected with named criteria, 1 developed to mid-fi *[Figma: White]*, with a 150-word rationale naming two trade-offs. | L# 2, 4, 7 |
| **Blue** | In 60 min: given an existing product flow, locate its inherent vs accidental complexity (Tesler), propose a redesign that moves one complexity off the user, and defend it in a 3-minute recorded talk that shows three options, ranked criteria and the cost of the choice. | L# 4, 5, 9 |
| **Brown** | In 2 h: a mis-framed brief from a "client" (a friend or an AI playing the role, briefed with a hidden real problem). Interview for 20 min, reframe, and present a direction and rationale. Pass = you uncover the hidden problem, and every question you asked was about past behaviour, not hypotheticals. | L# 1, 2, 5, 10 |
| **Black** | In a weekend: a complete mini-project (brief → research of 5 people → 3 directions → tested prototype *[Figma: Orange]* → case study) whose written rationale an experienced designer judges defensible at every decision, with a decision record for each one-way door. | L# 0–11 |

## 3. Drill Bank

Drills are short, repeated and timed. Delete or hide your previous answers before a rep: the point is production from memory, not a library you keep.

**D1 — Framing (rep until each is under target)**
| Drill | Target |
|---|---|
| Solution-brief → problem statement (who · situation · goal · obstacle) | 3 min |
| Name three stakeholders + each one's definition of success | 3 min |
| Write one measurable/observable success criterion for a given brief | 2 min |
| Five Whys from a symptom to a cause that is *not* "the user is careless" | 5 min |
| Three reframes of one brief, each implying a different product | 8 min |
| JTBD statement ("When I…, I want to…, so I can…") from a product you use | 2 min |
| HMW question: one too broad, one too narrow, one right, labelled | 4 min |
| Constraints inventory (time · money · tech · brand · legal · a11y · culture) for a random brief | 5 min |

Brief sources: pull a random app from your phone, a public sign, a government form, or any `TD-01` catalogue post, and invent the brief its designers were probably handed.

**D2 — Critique conversion**
| Drill | Target |
|---|---|
| Convert 5 reactions ("it feels off") into diagnoses with a mechanism | 5 min |
| Convert 5 directions ("make it bigger") into the problem they are trying to solve | 5 min |
| Four-part critique (objective · element · effect · why) of a random Dribbble shot | 6 min |
| Rose/Thorn/Bud on a screen of an app you used today | 5 min |
| Write the three clarifying questions you'd ask a vague piece of feedback | 3 min |
| Critique at fidelity: a wireframe (structure only, no visual notes allowed) | 6 min |

**D3 — Ideation bursts**
| Drill | Target |
|---|---|
| Crazy 8s: 8 distinct concepts, one per minute (paper or FigJam) | 8 min |
| SCAMPER: 7 concepts, one per letter, for a random everyday object | 10 min |
| Analogy transfer: solve a brief "as a hotel / an airport / a library would" | 8 min |
| Morphological matrix: 3 dimensions × 4 values, pick the 3 strangest viable combos | 10 min |
| Oblique Strategies card (random draw) → apply to the current build | 10 min |
| "Push past ten": list ideas 11–20 for a brief you've already done 1–10 for | 10 min |

**D4 — Trade-off articulation**
| Drill | Target |
|---|---|
| For a given design decision, name what was gained, what was given up, and for whom | 3 min |
| Satisfice or optimise? Classify 10 decisions in a project, with one-line reasons | 5 min |
| Inherent vs accidental complexity: sort 10 steps of a real flow | 6 min |
| Maeda's 10 laws: from memory, with one example each | 8 min |
| Rams' 10 principles: from memory, then argue against one for a given brief | 8 min |

**D5 — Self-critique protocols**
| Drill | Target |
|---|---|
| Thumbnail test: shrink your latest design to 150px wide and name what still reads | 2 min |
| Greyscale test (Figma: add a Saturation 0 fill layer on top) and list what vanishes | 3 min |
| Flip horizontally (Shift+H in Figma) and name the first balance error you see | 2 min |
| Stranger test: explain the purpose in one sentence to someone and note where they look first | 5 min |
| Read all copy aloud and mark every stumble | 4 min |

**D6 — Recall (flashcard-speed)**
Simon's definition · Alexander's fit/misfit · Rittel & Webber's wicked-problem properties (at least five) · Schön's reflection-in-action · Houde & Hill's three dimensions · Wallas's four stages · Norman's three levels of emotional design · Connor & Irizarry's four critique parts · Lerman's four steps · the five parts of a decision · the evidence ladder in order · Type 1 vs Type 2 decisions · the conditions on "five users" · *The Mom Test*'s three rules · Conway's law. **Target: each stated correctly in under 60 s, cold.**

**D7 — Decisions, users and teams** (L# 9–11)
| Drill | Target |
|---|---|
| Five-part decision record (question · ≥3 options · ranked criteria · choice · consequences) for a decision you made this week | 8 min |
| Classify 10 decisions in a live project as one-way or two-way doors, with the time each deserves | 4 min |
| Place 6 pieces of evidence on the evidence ladder and name the weakest rung you're relying on | 3 min |
| Rewrite 5 "would you…?" questions as "tell me about the last time…" questions | 4 min |
| Translate one recommendation into the criterion an engineer, a PM and a founder each care about | 5 min |
| Restate a hostile stakeholder question in terms of the user, then answer with a criterion | 3 min |

## 4. Exercise Bank

Graded exercises with acceptance criteria. Each one gets a short `NOTES.md`: what went wrong and what you'd do next time.

**Level 1 — Seeing design everywhere**
1. **Misfit safari.** Photograph 10 misfits in your hostel, campus or city (doors, signs, forms, queues). Per photo: the misfit, the probable cause, and a redesign sentence. *Accept:* no cause may be "people are stupid".
2. **Art / craft / design sort.** Collect 12 images (half from `TD-14`, half from your own feed) and place them on Kahoelk's Venn (`TD-13#21`) in FigJam with one-line reasons. *Accept:* at least two items placed in overlaps, with the overlap justified.
3. **Rationale reverse-engineering.** Take three well-known products and write the brief, constraints and success criterion their designers probably had. *Accept:* each brief is solution-free.
4. **Frustration log, week one.** Seven entries, mechanism named. *Accept:* three of seven name an affordance/signifier issue (L8-02 vocabulary preview).

**Level 2 — Framing under pressure**
5. **One brief, three products.** "Help students revise for exams." Three frames → three HMWs → three one-screen concepts *[Figma: White]*. *Accept:* someone shown the three screens can tell they solve different problems.
6. **Stakeholder map.** Your university's course-registration system: users, approvers, payers, affected non-users, each with a criterion and a conflict. *Accept:* at least two conflicting criteria identified and a proposed resolution.
7. **Success-criterion rewrite.** Take five vague goals ("make it intuitive", "modern look", "better engagement") and rewrite each as an observable criterion. *Accept:* each could be checked by a stranger.
8. **Market-context reframe.** Compare an Indian and a Western e-commerce product page in the same category (`TD-13#28`) and rewrite the brief for each market. *Accept:* at least three elements classified as necessary trust work vs clutter, with reasons.

**Level 3 — Diverge / converge**
9. **Forty thumbnails.** One brief (a poster for a campus event), 40 thumbnails in 40 minutes, then choose 3 against written criteria. *Accept:* the 3 chosen are not all from the first 10.
10. **Constraint remix.** Redesign the same poster under three invented constraints (one colour; type only; must work as a 1-inch sticker) *[Figma: White]*. *Accept:* each version is structurally different, not just recoloured.
11. **Arbitrary source.** Derive a visual system from a non-visual source (a raga's aroha–avaroha, a chess opening, a statute's structure), after `TD-03#6`. *Accept:* the mapping from source to form is written and consistent.
12. **Kill a darling.** Take your best recent piece, identify the element you're proudest of, remove it, and fix the design around the gap. *Accept:* a written note on whether the design improved and why.

**Level 4 — Critique and defence**
13. **Critique exchange.** Critique three strangers' work in writing (a design Discord, an r/design critique thread, or classmates) using the four-part structure. *Accept:* each critique names the objective before the element.
14. **Receive and triage.** Post your own work for critique, collect ≥5 notes, classify each as reaction/direction/critique, translate each into a diagnosis, act on the top two. *Accept:* the v2 addresses both, and the log records what you rejected and why.
15. **Three-minute defence.** Record yourself defending a design decision against a hostile question you wrote ("why not just use a carousel?"). *Accept:* the answer names a trade-off and a criterion, not a preference.
16. **Tesler redesign.** An existing multi-step flow (railway booking, a government portal). Map inherent vs accidental complexity and redesign one step to absorb complexity *[Figma: Yellow]*. *Accept:* the moved complexity is explicitly relocated (to defaults, to the system, or to a later step), not deleted by hand-waving.

**Level 5 — Philosophy in action**
17. **Philosophy swap.** Redesign one object Rams-style and one Memphis-style (Figma or sketch). *Accept:* a 200-word note on what each philosophy gained and lost for the users.
18. **Humanity-centred audit.** For a product you use, list who is affected but not designed for (non-users, workers, environment). *Accept:* one design change proposed that serves an excluded party without harming the core user.
19. **Write your one-page design philosophy v0.1.** *Accept:* at least five principles, each with a real example from your own work and a condition under which you'd break it.

**Level 6 — Decisions, users and teams** (L# 9–11)
20. **Decision records for EcoTrace.** Write records for three live EcoTrace decisions (e.g. data-entry method, dashboard hero, report format) in the five-part form. *Constraints:* criteria ranked before options are scored; at least one option is "the convention". *Accept:* a reader who wasn't there can say why each losing option lost, and each record names the test that would reopen it.
21. **Three-option presentation.** Present one design problem to a friend as three directions with ranked criteria, not one polished answer (L# 9). *Constraints:* 10 minutes, mid-fi only *[Figma: White]*. *Accept:* the discussion ends up being about the criteria ranking, and you record the ranking that emerged.
22. **Five past-behaviour interviews.** Interview five people who recently did the task your product supports (for EcoTrace: someone who sent a customer environmental or compliance data). *Constraints:* no questions about the future or about your idea until the last five minutes; ask to see real artefacts. *Accept:* ≥15 observations written as *person · action · reason*, clustered by workaround, and a rewritten problem statement.
23. **Workaround hunt.** Find five workarounds in one real workplace (a hostel office, a shop, a college department): sticky notes, private spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups. *Accept:* each workaround is linked to the complexity it absorbs (Tesler) and to one product requirement it implies.
24. **Scope negotiation role-play.** A friend or an AI plays an engineer who says your ideal design costs ten times the simple one (L# 11). *Constraints:* you may not simply insist or simply concede. *Accept:* you name the user outcome at stake, propose a staged version that protects it and define the measurement that decides whether to build the full version.
25. **Solo-hat audit.** Review your last month of EcoTrace or Meridian commits against your design files. *Accept:* a list of every place the build diverged from the design, each classified as "engineering constraint", "design improvement found in code" or "the engineer-hat overruled the designer-hat", with one reversal made.

## 5. Build Ladder

Real projects, in order. Each has a spec, a hard constraint (the part that forces the learning) and a stretch goal. A build is **done** when it has a problem statement, a success criterion, the explored directions (not just the winner), the final artefact and a written rationale.

| # | Project | Hard constraint | Stretch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Frustration Atlas** | 30 logged misfits, clustered by cause in FigJam into ≥5 named clusters | Publish as a one-page zine or web page |
| 2 | **Reframe portfolio piece** | Take one real, badly framed local service (hostel mess menu, library booking) and ship a reframed concept; must interview ≥3 users | Test the concept with 3 users and log misses |
| 3 | **The Three-Frame Poster** | One event, three posters from three frames; *[Figma: White]* only, no stock imagery | Print all three and put them up; observe which gets read |
| 4 | **Constraint Sprint** | Seven days, seven posters, each under a new invented constraint | Pick the best and write its full rationale |
| 5 | **Critique Circle** | Organise and run a four-person critique session using Lerman's process; you facilitate | Write the session up as a guide for the next facilitator |
| 6 | **Complexity Relocation** | A redesign of a real multi-step flow that measurably reduces user steps *[Figma: Orange, with prototype]* | Timed test with 5 people, before vs after |
| 7 | **Design Philosophy Manifesto** | One page, ≤10 principles, each defended with an example and a counter-case | Set it as a typeset poster (bridge to 04) |
| 8 | **Archive Study Series** | Six weekly one-hour archive visits, each producing one annotated plate | Turn the six into a talk (bridge to 26) |
| 9 | **The Rationale Portfolio** | Rewrite the case study of every past project in the five-line rationale format (L# 8) | Get one senior designer to read it and respond |
| 10 | **Flagship: a real problem, fully designed** | A problem from your own life (law school, Isha logistics, EcoTrace users) taken from frame to tested prototype | Ship it: working prototype, landing page, or printed artefact in use |

## 6. Critique & Failure Drills

Training for the part of design that costs the most: wrong direction discovered late. Each drill: set a 30–45 minute timer, work from the symptom, and write a postmortem naming the *mechanism*, not just the fix.

- **Mis-framed brief trial.** Take a brief that is secretly mis-framed (write ten for yourself in advance, or have an AI generate them with the real problem hidden in a sealed note). Find the real problem in 20 minutes of questions. Score: did you find it?
- **Solution-first autopsy.** Take a failed or abandoned project of yours, or a public product failure (Google Glass, Quibi, Juicero). Identify the moment the solution was chosen before the problem was framed.
- **Process-theatre detector.** Given a "case study" from a portfolio site, mark every artefact that is theatre (sticky-note photos with no decision attached) and every one that is evidence of a decision. Rewrite the case study keeping only evidence.
- **Hostile review.** An AI or a friend plays a sceptical creative director who attacks your latest build on five points. Respond in writing to each: accept, reject with reason, or reframe the question.
- **Sunk-cost trial.** Redo a project you spent a long time on, from the brief, in 10% of the original time. Compare, and name what the long version's hours actually bought.
- **Taste-gap diary.** When your work disappoints you, write exactly which gap (spacing, hierarchy, colour, concept, finish) and schedule the drill that targets it.

- **HiPPO trial.** Someone senior (played by a friend or an AI) overrules your design with a preference. In 20 minutes, write a reply that restates their concern in user terms, shows the evidence rung each side stands on and proposes a cheap test. Score it: did the reply move the argument from taste to criteria?
- **Leading-question audit.** Transcribe ten minutes of one of your interviews. Mark every question that was hypothetical, leading or about your idea, and rewrite each. Target: fewer than two per ten minutes by your fifth interview.
- **Decision-record autopsy.** Reopen a decision record from three months ago. Compare the predicted consequences with what actually happened, and name the criterion you mis-ranked.

- **Live review.** Paste a Figma/FigJam link to a build board (problem statement, explored directions, chosen direction, rationale) into a Claude chat: "Review against T8-01 [belt]: is the frame solution-free, are the directions different, does the rationale name trade-offs?" Log every finding as a failure with its mechanism.

**Postmortem template (five lines):** symptom · mechanism (where the reasoning went wrong) · why you didn't catch it sooner · the fix · the habit or check that prevents the class.

## 7. Study & Reproduce

**Monthly cycle:**
1. Pick one landmark design with a documented rationale (targets below).
2. **Before reading the rationale**, write the brief, constraints and success criterion you think its designers had, plus the three alternatives they probably rejected.
3. Read the real case study, interview or book chapter.
4. **Diff:** what did they see that you didn't? Which constraint did you miss? Which trade-off would you make differently, and why?
5. Reproduce one key artefact (a sketch sequence, a layout, a diagram) *[Figma: White]* to feel the decisions in your hands.

**Target list for the first year:** Harry Beck's London Tube map (1931) · Otl Aicher's Munich 1972 pictograms · Massimo Vignelli's NYC Subway map (1972) and its 2008 revision · the Eames India Report (1958) → NID · Braun SK 4 (Rams & Gugelot, 1956) · Apple iPhone launch keynote (2007): the framing of "three products in one" · Pentagram's rebrand case studies (pick two) · Muji's *Horizon* campaign and Kenya Hara's writing on it · Airbnb's 2014 Bélo identity and its critique · the Indian Railways IRCTC booking flow, against a rail app you consider excellent · Gov.UK's design principles and one service redesign · Figma's own UI3 redesign (2024), from their blog posts · a product team's public write-up of a reversed decision (GOV.UK's design and service blogs publish many): predict the evidence that caused the reversal before reading it.

## 8. Intuition Probes

Predict, write it down, then check. Track accuracy weekly: rising accuracy *is* rising intuition.

- **Framing probes:** before interviewing a user or reading a case study, predict the real problem in one sentence. Check against what you learn.
- **First-look probes:** before a stranger sees your design, predict where they'll look first and what they'll think it's for. Check.
- **Critique probes:** before posting work for critique, predict the three notes you'll get. Score how many you got right; misses reveal your blind spots.
- **Success probes:** before a test, predict the task completion rate and the step where people will stumble.
- **Idea-rank probes:** after a diverge session, rank your ideas before sleeping. Re-rank the next morning cold. Track how often your first ranking survives.
- **Trend-source probes:** when you see a "new" aesthetic, predict its historical source and decade, then verify (`TD-13#5`).
- **Consequence probes:** when you write a decision record, predict the one consequence users will notice first. Check it after shipping.
- **Interview probes:** before each interview, predict the workaround the person will show you. Misses are where your frame is wrong (L# 10).
- **Objection probes:** before a stakeholder review, predict the first objection and who will raise it. Track how often pre-wiring (L# 11) removed it.

## 9. Taste Forge

Weekly, 30–45 minutes. This is where you stop imitating and start deciding.

- **Pattern journal.** One entry per week: a design decision you made, the alternatives, why you chose it, and the conditions under which you'd choose differently. After six months this is your personal design handbook.
- **Three ways.** Solve one micro-brief three ways from three philosophies (functionalist, expressive, local-vernacular). Ship one, and write the paragraph defending it.
- **Your design philosophy.** The one-page document from Exercise 19, revised monthly. Every revision must be caused by a real project or a real critique, never by a reel.
- **Self-review.** Every month, review work from three months ago as if it were a stranger's. Write the four-part critique. Redo the top fix.
- **Input audit.** Monthly: list your top ten inputs by time spent. Replace at least one feed with an archive or a non-design source (`TD-13#15`, `#16`).
- **Constraint runs.** Monthly: one piece under a painful rule: one colour, one typeface, no images, 30 minutes, 1:1 square, or only Devanagari.
- **Decision diary review.** Monthly: reread the month's decision records and write one line on each: was the criteria ranking right, and would you decide the same way now?

## 10. Assessment & Graduation

You are **trained in design mindset** (for the purposes of this file) when all of the following are true, with artefacts in the folder:

1. **Framing fluency:** every D1 drill passes cold, within target, and your problem statements are solution-free.
2. **Critique fluency:** you can give and translate critique in the four-part form, and you have ≥10 written critiques of others' work and ≥5 triaged critique rounds of your own.
3. **Divergence:** three builds show ≥30 explored options, with the selection criteria written *before* the selection.
4. **Trade-off literacy:** every rationale you've written in the last month names what was given up.
5. **Intuition:** your framing and critique probe accuracy is above 70% over the last 30 probes.
6. **Philosophy:** your design-philosophy page exists, has been revised ≥3 times for stated reasons, and you can defend each principle with a project.
7. **Shipping:** Build 10 exists as something real that other people used, and its case study is written.
8. **Decisions and users:** ≥10 decision records exist, ≥5 past-behaviour interviews are logged with their workarounds, and one decision has been reversed on evidence you collected.
9. **Teaching:** you can explain framing, critique and the taste gap to a beginner, unrehearsed, in ten minutes each.

Anything not yet true names next month's programme.

## 11. Spaced Schedule

Mindset training works best threaded through the other Phase 1 modules, not in a block. Use this template and shift it to fit your week, keeping the ratio.

| Day | Session | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Drill (20 min) | One D1 framing drill, cold, on a new brief |
| Tue | Exercise (45 min) | Next exercise in the current level |
| Wed | Build (2 h) | Current build, one milestone, rationale updated |
| Thu | Drill (20 min) + Probe (10 min) | A D2 or D4 drill + three intuition probes |
| Fri | Trial (40 min) | Mis-framed brief or hostile review + postmortem |
| Sat | Rebuild (75 min) | Study & Reproduce target |
| Sun | Forge (40 min) + Log review (15 min) | Pattern journal, philosophy revision, weekly roll-up |
| Daily | 5 min | Frustration log entry, mechanism named |

**Review cadence:**
- **Weekly:** re-run one drill from two belts ago. If it's slower than target, it goes back into rotation.
- **Fortnightly:** reopen your oldest build and rewrite its rationale with what you know now.
- **Monthly:** self-review (Section 9), a fresh gate attempt, and the input audit.
- **Quarterly:** rewrite your design philosophy from scratch without looking at the old one, then diff.

**What goes into spaced repetition (Anki), facts only, never skills:**
- Definitions: Simon, Alexander (fit/misfit), wicked problems (Rittel & Webber), abduction, satisficing, reflection-in-action.
- Named lists: Rams' 10 · Maeda's 10 · Wallas's 4 · Norman's 3 levels · Houde & Hill's 3 · Lerman's 4 · Connor & Irizarry's 4.
- Attributions and dates: Bauhaus 1919–33 · Eames India Report 1958 / NID 1961 · Papanek 1971 · Venturi 1966 · Design Council Double Diamond 2004–05 / 2019.
- Framing formulas: problem statement · JTBD · HMW · success-criterion shape.
- Decisions and research: the five parts of a decision · the evidence ladder · Type 1 / Type 2 (Bezos, 2015) · Nielsen & Landauer (1993) and the five-user conditions · *The Mom Test*'s three rules · Conway's law (1968) · Nygard's decision records (2011).

**Two anti-patterns to watch in your own schedule:** consuming design philosophy (reading, reels) instead of applying it (breadth without production), and staying in framing because it feels safe while never shipping the visual (thinking without making). The belt gates exist to catch both.

## 12. Logbook

One file, `LOG.md`, append-only, one entry per session. Thirty seconds to write. It is the only record that shows whether the programme is working.

```
## 2026-10-06 · Exercise · 60 min · Ex. 22 interview 2 of 5 (EcoTrace)
Participant: accounts assistant at a small auto-parts firm.
Predicted workaround: a master Excel sheet. Actual: bills photographed and sent to the owner on WhatsApp, typed up at month end. Miss.
Leading questions: 3 in the first 10 min ("would it help if…"). Rewrote them for interview 3.
Rule learned: the data's first home is a chat, not a spreadsheet; the upload flow must start from a photo.

## 2026-09-28 · Drill · 20 min · D1: three reframes
Brief: "an app for the hostel mess". Target 8 min → took 13.
Frames: (1) knowing today's menu (2) reducing food waste (3) dietary needs.
Miss: first two frames were the same product in disguise (both "information").
Rule learned: a real reframe changes WHO benefits or WHAT success means.
Prediction: said users' main pain = menu visibility → interview said timing. Miss.

## 2026-09-30 · Trial · 40 min · Hostile review of Three-Frame Poster
5 attacks; accepted 2, rejected 2 (with reasons), reframed 1.
Mechanism: I defended the gradient because it took 2 h (sunk cost).
Fix: removed it; hierarchy improved (thumbnail test now reads the date).
```

**Fields, in whatever form suits you:** date · session type · duration · what · target vs actual · the *mechanism* of any miss · the rule extracted · prediction hit or miss.

**Monthly roll-up (15 minutes, appended to the same file):**
- Drills at target / drills attempted
- Probe accuracy this month (framing · critique · first-look)
- Recurring failure mechanisms (these become next month's drills)
- Builds advanced; builds stalled (and the honest reason)
- Philosophy revisions and what caused them
- The one thing to change next month

## 13. If You Only Have Thirty Days

A compressed run, for a sprint: 60–90 minutes a day, six days a week, run **alongside** modules 02–05, not instead of them.
